SOUL CEÓL · the music program

One mic in the middle of the room

Live music here isn’t a nightly event layered onto a bar — it’s what the building was chosen for. Traditional Irish sessions plus contemporary performances, five to seven nights a week on the plans, much of it played by musicians who live in this neighborhood.

What a “session” is

The tradition the pub is named for

A session is the communal tradition of musicians gathering to play together — tunes carried by ear, players trading the lead, the circle deciding what plays next. The pub calls itself Session House for that: “session” for the music, “house” for the warmth of a neighborhood home. The Irish word for the gathering is seisiún, and the Dorchester Reporter quoted the team’s plan in exactly that word: Irish pub grub “accompanied by Irish seisiún music — much of it by local musicians.”

The Burren model, in Adams Village

Civilized by design

“It’s basically what the Burren does in Somerville,” Frank Delaney told the licensing board, “where there could be one to five, ten musicians, but one general microphone in the middle to pick up… But it’s very civilized. We’re not talking about DJs and big bands.” One shared microphone keeps the music at conversation level: you can hear the set and the table next to you.

For families · for players

Bring the kids. Bring the fiddle.

“We’re really interested in having like a family-friendly restaurant that you can bring kids to see… live music. We’ll have traditional Irish music and some contemporary as well.”Teresa Murphy, managing partner, to the Boston Licensing Board

And for players: Delaney pointed the board at the neighborhood itself — “I’m sure living in the area you’re aware of how many musicians, Irish musicians live in this area, and how many musicians have reached out to us looking to play.” The room is being built for exactly that bench.
