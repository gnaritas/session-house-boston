Session House

An Irish pub built around one microphone in the middle of the room — trad sessions, contemporary sets, elevated pub grub, and a board that turns with the season. Come for the music. Stay for the soul.

How the room works

One microphone. Ten musicians. No stage.

Frank Delaney told the Dorchester Reporter what the room is built for: “It’s basically what the Burren does in Somerville — where there could be one to five, ten musicians, but one general microphone in the middle to pick up… But it’s very civilized. We’re not talking about DJs and big bands.”

01

Bring the instrument

Players from around the neighborhood gather with their instruments. “How many Irish musicians live in this area” — Delaney counted them before he built the room, and they’ve been reaching out to play.

02

Or just bring your ears

Managing partner Teresa Murphy wants a place families can bring kids to hear live music: trad sessions that carry the room without drowning it.

03

Trad plus contemporary

Traditional Irish sessions five to seven nights a week on the plans, with contemporary performances alongside — much of it by local players.

SOUL CEÓL · soul music

Music is the purpose. The building is the part after it.

SOUL CEÓL — “soul music” — is the pub’s own name for its theme: the connection between Irish music and the communities that keep those traditions alive. Adams Village is one of those communities, and the room is being finished in rich dark tones with warm gold accents and traditional musical imagery, so it reads like a place that’s always been there.

“Great music, great food, a great pint, a beautiful room — and most importantly, a sense that everybody is welcome at the table.”The founding team, via What Now Boston
