Trad · Dorchester

The seisiún, near you

There is a bench of Irish musicians around Adams Village — Delaney counted them when he was still testifying for the room. Session House is the pub being built for them: sessions five to seven nights a week on the plans, tunes by ear, one microphone doing the work.

How a session runs

Circles, not stages

Players gather close so they can hear each other — a fiddle, a box, a flute, a bodhrán, somebody’s daughter on vocals. Someone starts a set, the circle follows, and the next player leads the one after that. Nobody needs a soundcheck. That’s the tradition, and it’s why the pub named itself after it.

How the room holds it

One mic. Conversation level.

“One to five, ten musicians, but one general microphone in the middle to pick up… very civilized” — the room plan as Delaney described it to the board. The sound lifts off the circle, not off a PA stack, so the pint-and-talk layer of the night survives it.

The musicians are already here

Adams Village · Cedar Grove · the St. Brendan’s side of Dorchester

“I grew up in this area,” Delaney told the licensing board — Irish-born, first apartment on Adams Street, children and grandchildren at St. Brendan’s. He told board chair Kathleen Joyce he knew how many Irish musicians live in the area, and how many had already reached out wanting to play at Session House. The pub isn’t importing a concept; it’s opening a room for players who already live on these streets.
